# 0002 — We contest whether an activity happens, not whether it is material

- **Decided:** 2026-09-03
- **By:** Felix
- **Status:** agreed; the scoring consequence is still open (see below)
- **Touches:** scope 3 relevance, the four-pass assessment, what a determination may say

## The question

A company leaves a scope 3 category out and gives a reason. When are we entitled to disagree with
it, and on what grounds?

## What prompted it

H&M's 2025 report excludes capital goods with one sentence: *"No activities with relevant climate
impact have been identified for categories 2, 8, 9, 10, and 13 and these are therefore excluded from
disclosure."* The same report's cash flow statement shows SEK 10,679 m of capital assets acquired in
the year, and SEK 30,440 m of buildings, land, equipment, tools, fixtures and fittings on the balance
sheet.

Felix asked the right question before letting anyone call that wrong: *"are we really in a position
to discredit their relevance claim? Does 10bn spend mean it's defo material — what is the context of
that compared to their overall footprint?"*

## The decision

Felix, 2026-09-03: *"there is activity on the balance sheet so we disagree that there is no activity
unless it has been put into another emissions category. We can't make any claims on materiality but
we can on activities."*

So the line is drawn between two different claims a company can make:

- **"The activity does not happen here."** Testable against the company's own accounts and
  disclosures. If the accounts show the activity, the claim is contradicted by evidence, and we say
  so — unless the company shows the emissions accounted for under another category.
- **"The activity happens but is immaterial."** A judgement about size. We do not have the company's
  data, cannot compute their figure, and will not assert one. We do not contest this.

The GHG Protocol draws the same line, which is why this is a reading of the standard rather than a
house rule. Section 6.3 distinguishes categories that are *not applicable* — its example is a company
with no leased assets or franchises, told to report zero or "not applicable" — from activities a
company *has* but excludes, which "may [be excluded] provided that any exclusion is disclosed and
justified." H&M made the first kind of claim in a situation that is the second kind.

## What this means in practice

- An assessment may return a verdict of **gap** where the company says an activity is absent and its
  own reporting shows it is not.
- Before that verdict stands, the assessment must check the obvious alternative: has the company
  folded those emissions into another category? For H&M the only candidate is an "other expenditures"
  line inside category 1, described solely as spend-based with no statement of which spend it covers.
  The report neither confirms nor rules it out, and that is recorded rather than resolved.
- An assessment may **not** return a verdict that rests on our estimate of a company's emissions.
  Peer figures may be given as context — Inditex reports capital goods at about 3 percent of its
  scope 3, Chanel at 13 to 20 percent of hers — but they are context for a reader, never evidence
  that a particular company's number is large.

## Still open

What the score should do when a required category is excluded on a reason that does not hold.
Today the company drops to *unknown*, because scoring requires every material category to be
reported in three consecutive years. H&M scores 1.51 only because an override written months ago
waves capital goods through — an override whose recorded reason claims store fit-out investment "is
limited", which its accounts contradict, and adds that the emissions are captured in scope 1 and 2,
which confuses operating an asset with making it.

Felix parked this deliberately on 2026-09-03 — *"let's leave this for now and make sure all the
mechanics work end to end"* — so it is an open question, not an oversight. It has to be answered
before any company is published with a contested exclusion.
